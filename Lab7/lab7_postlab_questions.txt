Question 1: Where averaging stops helping (10 points)

a. Include your log-log plot of sigma against N with the sqrt(N) reference line.
   Over what range of N does your data follow the line, and at what N does it leave?

b. The two hundred-reading sets.
   Report both standard deviations: the fast one, and the one taken every 300 ms.
   Same sensor, same code, same sample count.
   Explain the difference in terms of how long the sensor takes to produce a genuinely new conversion,
   and say which of the two sets averaging could actually have helped.

c. Compare the flat region of your plot with one count of the 16-bit resolution, and with LSB/sqrt(12).
   Which is larger, and what does that tell you about what you were measuring once the curve went flat?

Part (b) is the one worth getting right.
Reading faster does not give you more information; it gives you the same information more times,
and averaging identical numbers achieves nothing at all.


Question 2: What integration time does (5 points)

a. Plot your NPLC sweep: standard deviation against integration time, on log axes.

b. At NPLC = 1 the meter integrates over exactly one mains period.
   Explain what that does to a 60 Hz interference signal, and why the reading is so much quieter there
   than at the shortest setting.
   Then say what the meter is doing to the signal that a single scope sample is not.


Question 3: The loop you built by accident (5 points)

a. Include both FFTs on common axes and report the amplitude at 60 Hz in each.
   Estimate the enclosed area of the two loops.
   The induced voltage is proportional to that area.
   Does the ratio of your two amplitudes match the ratio of your areas?

b. Is the 60 Hz line random noise, interference, or bias?
   Say which, and say what that means about whether averaging would ever have removed it.


Question 4: What the bus can really do (5 points)

a. Include your captured ACK edge.
   Report the 10-90% rise time and the fall time,
   and explain the asymmetry between them in terms of what is driving the line in each direction.

b. The rise is an RC charge through the pull-up.
   Extract the time constant from your data and, using the pull-up value from the schematic,
   compute the bus capacitance.
   The I2C specification allows a maximum rise time of 300 ns in fast mode
   and 1000 ns in standard mode.
   Which mode can your board actually support?
   Compare that with the maximum in the ADT7420 datasheet and say which of the two is the real limit.

c. You have now measured random noise, interference and at least one bias.
   Name one example of each from your own data this week,
   and state in a sentence what you would do about each.
   Only one of the three is helped by taking more readings.
